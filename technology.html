01The platform

Three properties, one mechanism.

The MEMS nebulizer amplifies small piezo vibrations step by step, so the liquid at the tip vibrates and pinches off. This phenomenon is known as Faraday-wave atomization.

With acknowledgement to the originators. The concept was invented by distinguished Professors Chen S. Tsai and Shirley C. Tsai at UC Irvine, explicitly for inhalation (2010, 2009, 2017). We build from that foundation.

02Why particle size matters

Only one narrow band reaches the deep lung.

A tight droplet distribution is necessary to ensure:

  1. Maximized bioavailability
  2. Increased dosing precision
  3. Minimal throat impaction, and therefore irritation
  4. Reduced drug waste

03Why low shear matters

Lipid nanoparticles are the sharpest case.

LNPs made RNA medicines possible, but the capsule does not survive conventional nebulization. Published work reports particle growth, aggregation and cargo loss from shear and interfacial stress (Wiebe 2026, Heiser 2025). Integrity largely holds below an energy-dissipation threshold of roughly 5–10 J/g (van Rijn 2023).

Solving inhaled biologics starts with the device.

04Built so far

From product vision to the bench.

Phase 1 is proof of concept and IP. What exists today is benchtop hardware: silicon resonator cells fabricated at wafer level, bonded to piezo blocks, and driven by a resonance-tracking system of our own.

Product vision

A pocket-sized, connected cartridge-and-device system.

A finger-sized inhaler with a snap-in liquid drug cartridge, paired with a companion app that guides the patient from prescription to first dose.

05Competition

A crowded field stuck on poor technologies.

We benchmark against vibrating mesh because it is the standard way to turn a liquid into a breathable aerosol. Mesh devices from Pari, Aerogen, Omron and Philips are good products — approved, at scale, refined over two decades. They push liquid through holes and keep making the holes better.

Besides being bulky, the vibrating-mesh nebulizer shears proteins hard enough to unfold and aggregate them, and a damaged dose provokes an immune response rather than merely underperforming (Lundahl 2021).

Dry powder is the other serious platform: expedient for pharma, but the complexity lands on users. Afrezza, a dry-powder insulin inhaler, was approved and proved a systemic biologic could be delivered through the lung — yet it never translated into market success.
