01Market

An urgent problem now, a new capability later.

We enter where drugs are already approved for inhalation and dose consistency and form factor are enough to compete — then open the lung to molecules that cannot be inhaled today.

Near term · Respiratory diseases 400M+ people affected by COPD alone

Asthma, COPD, cystic fibrosis

COPD is the third leading cause of death worldwide (Lancet Respir. Med., 2025), with cases set to rise 23% by 2050 (WHO/ERS). These drugs are already approved for inhalation; dose consistency and form factor are enough to compete.

Beyond respiratory · Systemic biologics $1T biologics market this decade, from $400–600B today

New possibilities create new markets

If a fragile molecule can be aerosolized intact, this becomes a new capability for pharma across the whole biologics universe (Mordor Intelligence).

02Where we are

From benchtop prototype to market launch.

  1. Move from benchtop prototype to MVP
  2. Validate the drug-stability claim against incumbent technologies
  3. Pharma partnership and clinical studies
  4. Build QMS and manufacturing line
  5. Market launch
  1. Phase 1In progress · 80%

    Proof of concept and IP. Benchtop prototype, patent filed, shear simulation. Aerosol observed on our own hardware, not yet characterised.

  2. Phase 2Next

    Alpha device. Handheld prototype and stability testing on a representative biologic.

  3. Go / no-goQ4 2028

    A stated decision on the platform thesis, judged against the evidence.

  4. Phase 3Planned

    Beta device and clinical. Validation on a partner molecule, the regulatory route and QMS implementation.

  5. Phase 4Target 2034

    First molecule launch. Manufacturing scale-up and market entry with the partner; medical-device and combination-product certification.

03Team

Why I left a good job to do this.

I have spent over ten years building inhalation devices, including a $200M programme that reached market successfully, and I know what separates a device that works on a bench from one that reaches market. When I understood what this architecture could do, I wanted to serve society better by becoming who I always wanted to be.

So I left a safe job and pressed on. Everything here — the prototypes, the patent, the simulation, the wafer runs — I paid for out of my own savings. The nights, the weekends and the risk are mine.

Bootstrapping carried the technology to where the remaining questions cost money to answer, and that is exactly what the seed round is for.
