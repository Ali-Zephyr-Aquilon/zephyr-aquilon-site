01Why now

A boom in new molecules. Delivery hasn't moved in decades.

AI is accelerating drug discovery and validation. A wave of new molecules and treatments is coming, for cancer, brain disorders, rare diseases and infectious diseases.

Yet how we administer them has not changed. Patients are given bulky, awkward devices — and the last thing they want is to display their hardship. It's time to upgrade the way we administer drugs by solving a major bottleneck.

Micro Electro-Mechanical Systems (MEMS) are tiny components — microphones, gyroscopes, camera optics — machined with techniques from the semiconductor industry. We use them to build a ground-breaking nebulizer for fragile medical biologics.

02The problem

The lung is the obvious route. It has stayed closed anyway.

Biologics are the fastest-growing class of medicine, and almost all are injected. Injectors are simply inconvenient: of 125,474 US adults newly prescribed a GLP-1, about half had discontinued within a year (Rodriguez 2025).

Why not a pill? Because only a fraction of orally administered large molecules survives the metabolism.

The lung is the obvious alternative, with a vascularized surface of 70–100 m² — about half a tennis court. Existing dry powder inhalers and nebulizers fail to deliver a satisfactory aerosol. That is not a verdict on inhalation; it is a verdict on the device.
