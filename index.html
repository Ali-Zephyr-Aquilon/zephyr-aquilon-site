01Why now

New molecules are booming. Delivery needs an upgrade.

AI is accelerating drug discovery and validation. A wave of new molecules and treatments is coming, for cancer, brain disorders, rare diseases and infectious diseases.

Yet how we administer them has not changed. Patients are given bulky, awkward devices and the last thing they want is to display their hardship. It's time to upgrade the way we administer drugs by solving a major bottleneck.

Delivery can be discreet, safe and precise.

02The problem

The lung is the obvious route. It needs the right device.

Biologics are the fastest-growing class of medicine, and almost all are injected. Injectors are simply inconvenient: of 125,474 US adults newly prescribed a GLP-1, about half had discontinued within a year (Rodriguez 2025).

Why not a pill? Because the gut is built to digest proteins: the oral bioavailability of peptides is typically below 1–2% (Drucker 2020).

The lung is the obvious alternative, with a vascularized surface of 70–100 m², about half a tennis court. Existing dry powder inhalers and nebulizers struggle to aerosolize fragile, high-dose biologics without damaging them. That is not a verdict on inhalation; it's a product and market brief.
